# Precision Agriculture and Sensor Technology: Evidence Base

This document summarizes published research findings on the measurable benefits of sensors, automation, and AI-driven control in controlled environment agriculture (CEA) and greenhouses. These are external benchmarks for evaluating whether precision technology delivers value.

## Water and resource efficiency
- Intelligent environmental control systems integrating sensors and automation have demonstrated successful water savings of 16.5% compared with conventional cultivation methods, while also improving plant health and yield (Kaya et al., 2025, Food and Energy Security).
- AI-driven irrigation scheduling in tomato greenhouses reduced water use by approximately 60% in multi-season trials that used real-time sensor data (MDPI Sensors review, 2025).

## Yield and quality
- Smart greenhouses that combine environmental sensors, IoT platforms, and AI-driven decision making have shown tangible improvements in both resource use and crop productivity across a structured review of 114 peer-reviewed studies (MDPI Sensors, 2025).
- Automated nutrient delivery (fertigation) systems enable precise application of nutrients to plant roots, improving nutrient uptake and reducing waste, which leads to improved crop yields and quality.
- Real-time data monitoring allows growers to manage light, temperature, humidity, and nutrients more accurately, improving crop growth efficiency.

## Strawberry-specific evidence (directly relevant to GreenLeaf)
- A 2025 University of Georgia / Purdue study (Frontiers in Plant Science) found that sensor-controlled fertigation, where soil moisture sensors trigger watering only when substrate moisture drops below a set threshold, improved yield and quality in greenhouse hydroponic strawberries compared with rigid fixed-schedule watering.
- This matters because most water management in CEA is based on a rigid schedule rather than the plant's actual changing water needs; sensor-based triggering closes that gap.

## The core mechanism
- The common thread across studies: precision technology replaces fixed rules of thumb and schedules with responsive, data-driven decisions. The benefit comes from acting on the plant's actual real-time condition rather than a calendar or habit.
- The optimal combination of air temperature, relative humidity, and light is what produces maximum yield, assuming CO2, pH, and nutrients are not limiting. Sensors make it possible to hold that combination consistently.

## Caveats and honest framing
- Many advanced control studies are validated only in simulation or at limited scale, not full commercial production. Reported gains vary widely by crop, climate, and baseline practice.
- The size of the benefit depends heavily on how poor the baseline (non-precision) practice was. A well-run conventional operation has less to gain than a poorly-run one.
