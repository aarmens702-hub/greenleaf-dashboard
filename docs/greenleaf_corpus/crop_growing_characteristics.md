# Growing Characteristics of GreenLeaf's Four Crops

GreenLeaf grows tomato, pepper, cucumber, and strawberry. This document summarizes general growing characteristics relevant to interpreting sensor and stress data for each.

## Tomato
- One of the three dominant Canadian greenhouse crops (31.6% of greenhouse fruit/veg area nationally).
- Sensitive to vapor pressure deficit (VPD): high VPD increases transpiration demand and can cause stress if irrigation and climate control don't keep up.
- Susceptible to tomato brown rugose fruit virus (ToBRFV), an ongoing challenge for BC, Ontario, and Quebec growers; it causes misshapen, discolored fruit and reduces yield, size, and flavor.
- Yield context: commercial greenhouse tomato can reach high annual yields (tens of kg per square metre per year) under optimal multi-cycle production.

## Pepper
- The second dominant Canadian greenhouse crop (30.3% of area). About half of BC's greenhouse vegetable acreage is in peppers and tomatoes.
- Longer crop cycle than cucumber; sensitive to temperature swings and light levels during fruit set.
- Also affected by trade-sensitive virus restrictions (rugose virus restrictions cover pepper seed and fruit).

## Cucumber
- The third dominant Canadian greenhouse crop (29.6% of area); accounts for the largest share of greenhouse vegetable export value (38%).
- Fast-growing, high-transpiration crop; very responsive to humidity and water management.
- Short cycle means multiple harvests per year are common.

## Strawberry (GreenLeaf's dominant crop)
- A smaller but fast-growing segment of Canadian CEA (strawberry area grew 8.1% in 2024).
- Commonly grown in soilless hydroponic systems using inert substrate and nutrient solution.
- Research shows sensor-controlled fertigation (watering triggered when substrate moisture drops below a threshold) improves yield and quality versus rigid fixed-schedule watering.
- Strawberry water management has historically relied on fixed schedules rather than the plant's actual changing needs, making it a strong candidate for precision irrigation gains.

## Cross-crop note on stress signals
- Across all four crops, the key environmental stress drivers tracked in GreenLeaf's data are VPD, substrate moisture, substrate EC (nutrient strength), canopy temperature relative to air temperature, and pest/disease pressure.
- A canopy temperature that runs hotter than air temperature is a common early stress signal: a stressed plant that can't transpire enough to cool itself heats up before other symptoms appear.

## Relevance to GreenLeaf
GreenLeaf is unusually strawberry-weighted (about 57% of plots) compared with the national tomato/pepper/cucumber dominance. The strawberry fertigation research is therefore directly applicable: GreenLeaf's precision irrigation actions on strawberry should, in principle, show the yield/quality benefits documented in the literature. This is a testable claim the dashboard can examine.
