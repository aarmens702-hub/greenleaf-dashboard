# Agricultural Labor Costs and Shortages

Labor is one of the largest cost categories in greenhouse production and a constraint the GreenLeaf case explicitly highlights. Understanding labor economics is key to the value case for precision technology, because precision tools help direct scarce labor to where it matters most.

## How big is labor as a cost?
- For greenhouse and nursery operations, wages and salaries plus contract labor represented about 42% of production expenses (2022 US Census of Agriculture), compared with only 12% for all farms.
- For some specialty crop and greenhouse growers, labor costs reach nearly 40% of overall expenses, forcing hard decisions like cutting less-profitable crops.
- Labor is therefore on the same scale as energy as a major greenhouse operating cost.

## The Canadian labor shortage
- Canada's farm labor deficit was projected to roughly double to 123,000 workers (about one in three jobs) by 2029, according to the Canadian Agriculture Human Resource Council.
- An earlier shortage of 16,500 workers cost Canadian farmers an estimated 2.9 billion dollars in lost revenue.
- Agriculture has the highest job-vacancy rate of any major sector in the Canadian economy.
- Farms rely heavily on temporary foreign workers (Seasonal Agricultural Worker Program, Agri-Food Pilot); foreign labor accounts for roughly 17% of the sector's employees.

## Labor-intensive crops are hardest hit
- Strawberries, tomatoes, and leafy greens are particularly affected because they require intricate hand labor.
- This is directly relevant to GreenLeaf, whose dominant crop (strawberry) is labor-intensive.
- In surveys, a majority of farmers cite labor shortages as their top operational challenge.

## Climate compounds the labor problem
- Extreme weather increases labor needs (more work to deal with new pests and weather pressure) while reducing labor availability (workers need more breaks or days off during extreme heat).

## Why this matters for precision technology
- When labor is scarce and expensive, the value of precision tools rises: they help managers decide where to send limited crews first (triage), avoid wasted trips, and catch problems early before they require more labor to fix.
- This is the core of the "weekly alert triage" use case: with limited Monday-morning labor, which plots should the crew visit first?

## Relevance to GreenLeaf
GreenLeaf's daily cost data includes daily_labor_cost, and its actions are tagged precision vs. routine. Because labor is both expensive and scarce, precision actions that focus labor on the highest-need plots (rather than blanket routine work) are a direct efficiency lever. The dashboard's "plots needing attention" ranking is essentially a labor-allocation tool.
