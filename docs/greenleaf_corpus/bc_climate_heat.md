# BC Fraser Valley Climate and Extreme Heat

The Fraser Valley is one of Canada's three main greenhouse regions and the likely setting for a BC CEA operator like GreenLeaf. Its climate, and increasingly its extreme heat events, directly shape greenhouse stress and the value of responsive climate control.

## Normal growing-season climate
- The Fraser Valley has a moderate oceanic climate: mild wet winters, warm relatively dry summers.
- Typical summer highs reach about 20-26°C; winter temperatures generally range 2-8°C.
- The region gets most of its 1,200-1,800 mm of annual precipitation between October and March.
- Its southern latitude and maritime influence give it a longer growing season than most of Canada.
- About 40% of Fraser Valley agricultural land is irrigated to maintain crop health during dry summers; dry-year water demand has been nearly double (192%) that of wet years.

## The warming trend
- The Fraser Valley has been warming faster than nearby Vancouver, with real consequences for its billion-dollar agriculture industry.
- Climate projections call for increasing temperatures across all seasons, with summer warming slightly more.
- A longer, warmer growing season could permit higher yields and more diverse crops, but also raises heat-stress risk for crops sensitive to high temperatures, and increases pest and disease pressure.

## Extreme heat: the 2021 heat dome
- In late June 2021, BC experienced a record-shattering heat dome. Lytton, BC recorded 46.1°C, the highest temperature ever recorded in Canada, breaking the previous national record.
- Abbotsford (in the heart of Fraser Valley greenhouse country) hit around 42°C.
- Fraser Valley raspberry and other crops suffered massive failures, with temperatures spiking roughly 20°C above safe growing temperatures for some fruit.
- Cooling systems that normally worked failed above ~41°C in some operations.
- Such events are becoming frequent enough that authorities have raised the threshold for issuing heat warnings.

## Why this matters for precision technology
- Extreme heat is exactly the scenario where early warning and fast response have the highest value: a plot heading toward heat stress can sometimes be saved by timely venting, shading, or irrigation if caught early.
- This supports the "at-risk plots before a heatwave" scenario: sensors that detect rising VPD and canopy-temperature divergence can flag stress before it becomes catastrophic.

## Relevance to GreenLeaf
GreenLeaf's sensor data tracks air temperature, canopy temperature, VPD, and a plant stress index daily. The canopy-minus-air temperature gap is an early heat-stress signal. In a region prone to sudden extreme heat events, a precision system that flags at-risk plots days ahead, and a team that responds same-day, is the difference between a manageable stress episode and a crop failure. This is the strongest real-world grounding for GreenLeaf's "early warning" value story.
