# Fertilizer and Input Price Trends

Input costs, especially fertilizer, are volatile and have a large effect on farm margins. The GreenLeaf case emphasizes that input costs swing week to week. This document provides external context for those swings.

## Fertilizer price volatility
- After Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, Canadian fertilizer prices spiked: the Farm Input Price Index rose 18.4% year-over-year from Q2 2021 to Q2 2022, with fertilizer up 80.8% and machinery fuel up 78.5%.
- Prices then partly reversed: fertilizer fell about 21% year-over-year by Q2 2023.
- By 2025 prices remained well above historical lows and trended upward again. Phosphate (DAP) rose from about USD 583/ton in January 2025 to nearly USD 800 in August 2025, a 36% increase in under eight months.
- The Canadian nitrogen fertilizer price index showed annualized growth of about 6.7% over 2020-2025.

## Why fertilizer prices swing so much
- Natural gas is the primary feedstock for nitrogen fertilizer, so nitrogen prices track energy markets.
- Global trade and geopolitics (the Ukraine war, tariffs on Russian and Canadian fertilizer) drive sharp, hard-to-predict swings.
- US corn acreage strongly influences nitrogen demand and therefore price.

## The margin squeeze
- For 2026, crop prices were forecast to decline while input costs trended back up, squeezing margins. The fertilizer-to-crop price ratio (cost of fertilizer relative to expected crop revenue) is a key affordability measure and was projected to worsen slightly.
- With margins under pressure, managing controllable costs becomes more important. Decision aids and agronomic advice that improve input efficiency directly help.

## Why this matters for precision technology
- When fertilizer is expensive and volatile, applying exactly the right amount at the right time (precision fertigation) protects margin more than blanket scheduled application.
- This connects to GreenLeaf's High N vs Low N vs Control treatments: testing whether more nitrogen actually pays, rather than assuming "more nitrogen = more yield," is exactly the kind of evidence-based decision that matters most when nitrogen is costly.

## Relevance to GreenLeaf
GreenLeaf's data includes a market_and_input_prices table (fertilizer, pesticide, energy, water prices over time) and daily_fertilizer_cost in the cost data. Because fertilizer prices are volatile and rising, the value of precision nutrient management rises too. The High N / Low N treatment comparison lets GreenLeaf test the common "high nitrogen for more yield" rule of thumb against real profit outcomes, which is especially valuable when every extra kilogram of nitrogen costs more.
