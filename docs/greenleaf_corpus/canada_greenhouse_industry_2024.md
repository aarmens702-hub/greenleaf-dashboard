# Canadian Greenhouse Vegetable Industry Overview (2024)

## Industry scale
- Canada's greenhouse area for fruits and vegetables expanded by nearly 1 million square metres to reach 23.4 million square metres (about 252 million square feet) in 2024.
- Total greenhouse area for all products grew from 305 million square feet in 2020 to 359 million square feet in 2024.
- Nationally, about 70% of greenhouse area was used for fruit and vegetable cultivation; the remainder was ornamentals.
- The greenhouse fruit and vegetable sector reached a total farm-gate value of 2.7 billion dollars in 2024.

## Crop mix
- Tomatoes (31.6%), peppers (30.3%), and cucumbers (29.6%) together accounted for the majority of greenhouse-grown fruits and vegetables in 2024.
- These three crops made up 91.9% of the total farm-gate value of all greenhouse fruits and vegetables.
- Sales of tomatoes, peppers, and cucumbers rose 8.5% to reach 2.5 billion dollars in 2024.
- Strawberry growing area grew 8.1% and lettuce area grew 8.0% in 2024, though they remain a small share of the total.

## Costs
- Greenhouse operating expenses increased 4.3% to 4.0 billion dollars in 2024.
- The main cost drivers were higher spending on electricity (up 8.3%) and plant materials (up 8.0%).

## Geography
- Most greenhouse facilities are concentrated in southern Ontario, the Fraser Valley in British Columbia, and the Montreal area in Quebec.
- Ontario leads the greenhouse sector. British Columbia's Fraser Valley is one of the three major Canadian greenhouse regions.

## Workforce
- Employment in greenhouse fruit and vegetable production rose from 13,900 in 2020 to 15,900 in 2023.

## Relevance to GreenLeaf
GreenLeaf CEA operates in BC and is heavily weighted toward strawberry production (about 57% of its experimental plots), which is unusual relative to the national crop mix where tomato, pepper, and cucumber dominate. This makes GreenLeaf a useful test case for whether precision techniques transfer to a less conventional crop mix.
